# MOB — Kickstarter narrative and production blueprint

Controlling revision 3 · 11 September 2026 · Not a live campaign · No pledges or preorders open

## Campaign premise

**Your AI. Its own computer.**

MOB is a pocket AI computer being designed to run a local model, speech, working memory, a browser and command tools. Voice starts the work. An iPhone companion provides setup, review and control. Supported connections extend tasks to the devices and programs the user approves.

The vision covers research, making useful documents, working with files and running supported tasks. A client reply is one demonstration. Technical builders, founders and independent professionals are possible research cohorts; no cohort, demand or repeated-use behavior is validated today.

Current images show design intent. There is no validated integrated hardware prototype, measured battery result, announced delivery date or final production economics. This document plans the future campaign; it is not publishable proof that the product works.

## Narrative available for concept-stage communication

Your work moves through files, browsers and computers. Your attention connects them.

We are designing MOB to give your AI a computer of its own: a place to work with context, use tools and prepare something useful from a spoken request. Your iPhone would show what it used, what it did and what needs your decision.

Ask it to research a choice. Prepare a document from a folder you select. Run an approved command on a connected workstation. Each action should have a clear scope and a result you can inspect. Remote access requires supported software and operating-system permission; a network connection alone does not unlock applications.

The object matters too: controls you can feel, clear status, a serviceable USB-C lead and a battery sized for a useful mixed-workload day. Magnetic mounting, wireless charging and Bluetooth call assistance are engineering targets. We must prove the local workflow, power, thermals, audio and user control on real hardware before promising them to backers.

| Chapter | Question to answer | Required evidence |

| The physical experience | How do I operate and power it? | Real microphone cutoff, separate task stop, cable, charging/mounting and supported servicing |
| Production | How will you deliver it? | Costed BOM, written partner scope, build/test plan, tooling and certification state |

| The team | Why can this team complete the work? | Actual roles, relevant experience, gaps and accountable partners; no invented shipping history |

## Demonstration footage requirements

Record a continuous, uncut core demonstration on the actual prototype. Show the device, source of voice input, network state, selected files, tool action, iPhone review and real output. Keep hardware/software versions and the original recording. Do not replace waiting or failures with synthetic working footage.

Capture at least one successful local task and one cancellation or permission failure. A web task needs its internet connection explained; an offline local test must not secretly depend on a cloud model. A remote task must identify the computer and the specific allowed command. Show mute and task cancellation separately. Publish test conditions and limitations beside future measured claims.

Call assistance belongs in the promised scope only after a real phone/OS combination passes duplex audio, echo control, routing and user takeover. If it fails the gate, disclose it as an excluded future target rather than a funded reward feature. Do not imply universal call coverage.

Battery footage and results need the capacity, cell age, software build, ambient temperature, duty cycle, cutoff and continuous-load behavior. Half-day readiness and continuous local inference are different workloads.

## Two-minute story film plus uncut proof

| Time | Real footage | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–15 s | Founder and a real task spanning resources | Introduce the job and the physical-computer thesis |
| 15–35 s | Actual prototype in hand and phone companion | Make scale, controls and development stage clear |
| 35–70 s | Continuous core task segment | Show the request, scoped execution and real output |
| 70–95 s | Approved workstation action and a failure/cancellation case | Demonstrate reach and control with actual results |
| 95–110 s | Power/audio/assembly evidence that has passed | State supported features and remaining limitations |
| 110–120 s | Founder and engineering bench | Explain what funding completes and the concrete invitation |

The edited story film must not masquerade as the uncut demonstration. Publish the complete proof recording separately at its natural duration; label any cut or speed change in the story film. Use the founder's real voice and prototype sound. Generated concept material belongs to the labelled prelaunch/investor context, not as substitute hardware proof on the funding page.

## Rewards, goal and production economics

Plan one deliverable baseline device. Consider a pilot/developer edition only when its scope and support can actually be fulfilled. Add accessories only after their engineering and economics are settled. Avoid stretch goals that create new hardware dependencies before the baseline is delivered.

No retail price, gross-margin target, quantity cap, funding goal or shipping estimate is approved here. Build the funding requirement from remaining development, tooling, certification, a viable minimum batch, landed unit costs, packaging, fulfillment, support/returns, platform/payment costs, taxes and contingency, less secured funds actually assigned to those costs. Test willingness to pay and reconcile real supplier quotes before setting rewards. A small public goal must not conceal unfunded manufacturing obligations.

Before launch, disclose actual prototype functions and unsupported functions, manufacturing ownership and written scope, DFM/DFT status, sourcing/alternates, test coverage, traceability, repair/support plans and shipping territories. Name a supplier as a partner only with agreement and permission. Funding is not secured merely because a pitch has been sent.

## Creator eligibility gate

Kickstarter's official hardware rules require working prototypes, prohibit photorealistic renderings in hardware/product-design campaigns and require an explanation of production and relevant experience. Treat the current cinematic concept images as prelaunch assets. Recheck the platform rules before submission. [Kickstarter hardware rules](https://help.kickstarter.com/en-us/articles/16236428-what-are-the-rules-for-hardware-and-product-design-projects), checked 11 September 2026.

India is absent from Kickstarter's current creator-country list. MOB needs a legitimate qualifying individual or entity structure, authorized representative, beneficial-owner/identity and tax verification, and a supported bank account meeting the platform requirements. Establishing an eligible business may provide a route, but incorporation alone is not completed verification. Do not borrow an identity or falsify the project location. [Kickstarter eligibility](https://help.kickstarter.com/en-us/articles/16236650-who-can-use-kickstarter), checked 11 September 2026.

## Release gates

1. Integrated prototype with complete, real demonstration recordings and disclosed limitations.
2. Local-task, thermal, power, cancellation and permission evidence; call evidence for any call promise.
3. Costed BOM, sourcing plan and written assembler/engineering scope with viable funding.
4. Appropriate product/radio/battery/transport test and certification plan, with actual status stated.
5. Legitimate eligible creator/entity, bank and platform verification completed.
6. Reward contents, prices, caps, territory terms and schedule supported by operations.
7. Real prototype photography/footage and a claim-to-evidence review of every public statement.
8. Customer support and regular progress-update process with a response plan for scope or schedule changes.
